Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have your roof checked annually and fix vulnerable areas ahead of the stormy months
  • Take dated photos of the house every year, since insurers ask about prior condition
  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Install impact-resistant windows or storm shutters if you live in a hurricane-prone area
  • Extend downspouts and slope the soil so runoff moves away from the foundation
  • Make sure the attic is properly ventilated and insulated to prevent ice dams

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain
  • Damp, earthy odors that appear a few days after the storm
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Standing water in attic spaces, especially by skylights and chimneys
  • Water getting past doors, windows and wall penetrations when wind pushes the rain

What a Professional Brings to Storm Damage Repair

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Temporary protection such as tarps and board-up needs correct technique or it will not hold. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load.
